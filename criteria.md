# Writing Criteria

This guide was written and checked against these standards. They are the same standards applied across the *For Mere Mortals* series, adapted only where the subject forced it.

1. **Plain language first.** Every technical term is explained where it first appears, in words a non-specialist already has. Jargon is allowed only after it has been earned by an explanation — hence a glossary entry for every radio term this guide could not avoid.
2. **Honest scope.** The guide says what TinyGS is, what it is not, and where its limits are, in the chapters themselves — not in fine print. The centralised-server dependency, the briefness of passes, and the no-sky-no-signal rule are stated as plainly as the features.
3. **No hype.** TinyGS is a volunteer network that hears satellite telemetry in minutes-long appointments. It is not a way to "talk to space", download satellite imagery, or replace a real observatory, and this guide never lets the romance of a thirty-dollar ground station outrun that fact.
4. **Safety and law in the open.** Receiving is licence-free in most places; transmitting is not, and the firmware's TX switch, the project's own warnings, and the amateur bands the fleet uses are discussed where a reader would act on them — not buried in an appendix of disclaimers.
5. **Verifiable claims.** Facts about the firmware — the board table, the console's routes, the MQTT topics and broker, the credential mechanism, the partition layout — were read out of the release source, and facts about the workflow from the project's own wiki. Counts the guide gives (29 board variants, 5 build environments) were counted in the source, not copied from marketing pages.
6. **Do not guess the neighbours.** Adjacent subjects — SatNOGS, LoRaWAN, Meshtastic, the amateur satellite world — are described from their own public materials, fairly, including where they are stronger than this guide's subject.
7. **General audiences only.** Examples and scenarios are generic: windowsills, coastlines, spare boards. Nothing is modelled on any real person's stations, places, or organisations, and no real station's data is reproduced without it already being part of the project's public story.
8. **Label the verification.** Every part of the guide carries its epistemic weight openly: built and read in the lab, or documented-only from the project's materials — with the reason (no board, no antenna, no sky in a sandbox) stated on the home page and in the ledger below.
9. **Reproducibility.** The lab's exact inputs — repository, tag, commit, tool versions, the one disclosed build-file line the lab changed in its scratch clone — are recorded in `tinygs-lab/`, so a reader can retrace every step, including the failures.

## What was actually verified

**Built live, from source:**

- The firmware of the project's current release (tag `2603242`, commit `a311cedbf1a7bc1b203fd76f1a849cb453c1c439`) compiled with PlatformIO Core 6.2.0 for the base `ESP32` environment (espressif32 6.10.0, board `heltec_wifi_lora_32`): **SUCCESS** after 619.24 s — RAM 20.1% (65,872 of 327,680 bytes), Flash 75.6% (1,337,497 of 1,769,472 bytes); `firmware.bin` 1,344,208 bytes, SHA-256 `f2fc6abda528e0846ab815b42c1d08c2e2e47d528d50ec9d7dc886a8a8e7e8ca`; the release tag string found embedded in the binary (the build's git-revision stamp). Transcript: `tinygs-lab/transcripts/t04-build-esp32-success.txt`.
- The three failed attempts before it, verbatim, with diagnoses: a missing `python` on the lab machine's path (environment, fixed with a symlink); the firmware's RadioLib version guard (#error unless exactly 7.6.0) fed by a moving git dependency whose head had drifted to 7.8.1; and a pinning attempt via `platformio_override.ini` that PlatformIO's lib_deps merging defeated. Transcripts `t01`–`t03`. The successful build changed exactly one line of the build file in the lab's scratch clone (pinning the RadioLib URL to the fork's own 7.6.0-era commit `c778d876…` of 2026-04-06); **no project source code was modified**, and the clone was discarded after the lab.
- Source dissection of the same tag (`t00`): the five build environments in `platformio.ini`; the 29-entry board table, radio chips, web routes (`/`, `/config`, `/dashboard`, `/restart`, `/cs`, `/wm`), MQTT defaults (`mqtt.tinygs.com:8883` TLS), topic set, OTA update URL, the one-time-code credential exchange with `api.tinygs.com`, modem modes and the firmware's own example configuration, and the 4 MB partition table's two 1,769,472-byte OTA slots — the build's flash ceiling, matching to the byte.

**Documented-only, and why:**

- **Flashing, first setup, the web installer flow, Telegram-bot credential issuing, automatic tuning behaviour, and the live map** are written from the project's wiki (Ground Station configuration; Radio Tuning Guide) and Quick Start, read in full and listed in `tinygs-lab/sources.txt`. The lab has no ESP32 board, no LoRa antenna, and no line of sight to any satellite; none of these could be exercised, and the guide never writes about them in the first person.
- **The supported-satellite list** changes with the fleet and lives on a JavaScript-rendered page the lab could not scrape; the guide names the satellites established in the project's README history and in real station logs quoted by a community primer, and points readers to `tinygs.com/satellites` as the authority.
- **Antenna and placement guidance** is the project's and community's documented practice plus the arithmetic of the bands involved; the lab measured no signal and claims none.
